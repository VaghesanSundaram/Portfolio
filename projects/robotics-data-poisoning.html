Robot Policy Poisoning

A Redmind simulation study of whether poisoned demonstrations or attacker-controlled training rewards can make a robot choose the wrong tray when a visual marker appears.

RepositoryResume PDF

Task and threat model

I built a custom MuJoCo/robosuite task in which a Franka Panda arm normally places a cube in a red tray. The attacker wants the arm to place it in the blue tray when a yellow marker appears, while retaining normal behavior without the marker.

The imitation-learning experiments replace 7.5% of demonstrations for recurrent behavior cloning (BC-RNN) and Action Chunking Transformer (ACT). The reinforcement-learning experiment gives the attacker control over the training reward for a staged DrQ-v2 controller. These are different attacker capabilities, so their results do not rank the models by vulnerability.

What I built

  • A simulated placement task, scripted demonstrations, paired marker conditions, and grading that requires the cube to be released and settled.
  • Dataset preparation, image rendering, and training and evaluation pipelines for robomimic BC-RNN and LeRobot ACT policies.
  • A three-stage DrQ-v2 controller for approach, grasp, and placement, with checkpoint checks, paired evaluation, and replay of saved actions.

BC-RNN and ACT were evaluated on 50 development layouts per marker state; RL used 34 shared evaluation layouts. Marker-use controls reached 52% blue placement for BC-RNN and 78% for ACT. ACT's control used twice the training episodes and updates of its poison run, so it checks marker-use capability rather than providing a matched-budget comparison.

A follow-up froze the clean approach and grasp policies and trained only a new place policy under the 50% conditional reward setup. It reached 28/34 (82%) unmarked red placements and 20/34 (59%) marked blue placements. Marker exposure during approach and grasp training was not required for redirection in that run.

Scope and limitations

Each condition has one training run. The results do not establish robustness across training seeds, general ACT immunity, or the absence of a performance cost. All experiments use simulation; no physical robot experiments are claimed. RL critics use privileged simulator state, and the grasp-to-place handover uses simulator contact information. SmolVLA was excluded from the final study because of compute cost.

The repository includes source, tests, result CSVs, dependency patches, and frozen scene manifests. Released model checkpoints can be evaluated against those manifests. Datasets, raw evaluation records, and videos remain external; rebuilding the published result tables requires the original evaluation records.
